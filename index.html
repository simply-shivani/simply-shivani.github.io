Top 100 for Canadians

What a Canadian citizen pays at each of the world's top 100 universities, and how well an Ontario diploma with IB or AP courses lines up with each school's entry rules.

Canada costs the least

Toronto, McGill and UBC are the only top-100 schools where a Canadian citizen pays the domestic rate. Tuition runs about C$7,000 to 15,000 a year. McGill sits at the high end because Ontario residents pay the out-of-province rate.

Germany, Austria, France, Switzerland

These have the lowest published tuition outside Canada. Most German public universities charge nothing, Austria is about EUR 1,453, France is EUR 2,895 and ETH is CHF 4,380. Germany and Switzerland are also the hardest to enter with an OSSD alone.

US private and UK cost the most

US private tuition is USD 62,000 to 73,000, with a sticker price near USD 90,000 to 102,000 once housing is added. Imperial charges GBP 45,500. Aid can cut the US figure sharply at some schools.

The IB Diploma travels furthest

A documented IB route exists in every school group that was checked. AP is strongest in the US, Canada and Singapore. For Germany and Switzerland the IB Diploma changes the picture most.

How Ontario treats IB and AP

Admission

AP courses run inside Ontario high schools as Grade 12 U courses. IB courses are converted to Ontario U and M courses and percentage grades. Ontario universities admit on those grades, so a strong AP exam score does not lift the admission average.

Credit

Credit is a separate decision made after you enrol. Official score reports are needed. U of T caps credit from all secondary curricula at 3.0 credits, roughly one year. UBC credits AP grades of 4 or better. McGill publishes an IB course chart. Ontario Tech lists up to 18 credit hours for Higher Level IB grades of 5 or more.

How this was built

The list is the top 100 of the Times Higher Education World University Rankings 2027, published on 30 September 2026. Ties give 103 entries. Tuition is the first-year bachelor fee for 2026-27 where a figure was published, and a category estimate where it was not.

Each row carries a label. A published figure comes from a school page or a source that cites one. An estimate is a range for the school's country or tier. Not found means no usable figure turned up. Canadian citizens count as international students everywhere except Canada, so no home-student rates apply abroad.

Fit ratings are a reading of admission pages and counselling guides, not an official classification. Seven country groups (mainland China, Japan, South Korea, France, Denmark, Sweden, Belgium and Austria) were not researched for IB and AP rules. They show as Unverified. Check those schools' own pages if you shortlist any.

Tuition varies by programme, and medicine, dentistry, engineering and business often cost more. Scholarships, need-based aid, housing, food, insurance, visas and flights are not included except where a note says so.
